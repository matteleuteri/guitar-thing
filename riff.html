Notation

How to write this — syntax, the bar ruler, and what plays
  • A chord line is one chord per bar, read left to right. Spaces or | both work, and the spacing is yours to choose — see the bar ruler below.
  • A tab lane is LANE| plus the notes, one per character column: e|5---7---|. Lanes are e B G D A E.
  • They play together, not instead of each other. A chord and a tab note on the same beat sound as one moment: the chord with the note on top of it, exactly as you'd strum it. The "What you'll hear" list spells that out as C + 5 | xx201x | E3 G3 C4 with A4 on top.
  • The bar ruler above the box is the shared grid: each number sits at the character where that bar starts, two characters along (a tab column starts after the e|). Pad the chord line so its tokens land on those numbers and both layers line up — it changes nothing you hear, it just makes the alignment visible. C Am F G and C       Am are the same four chords.
  • Space, -, . and _ are rests. x is a muted string. A two-digit fret uses two columns, so 10 is wider than 9.
  • Every lane must be the same number of characters — pad the short ones with dashes, or a bar shifts sideways.
  • tempo 96 — bpm (bpm also works).
  • bar 4 — beats per bar. Only needed to change 4/4.
  • step eighths — how long one column lasts. Take it from your fastest note: quarter, eighths, sixteenths, eighth triplets, or a plain number of beats like 0.25. The reader names the grid back at you.
  • strum 55 — ms between strings. 0 blocks the chord.
  • release 400 — damp each note early. Omit it to let the ring, which is the default.
  • Everything is optional except the notes. Lines starting with # are comments.

A chord name is pitch classes only — C does not carry frets, so the app searches for a shape. Use Chord voicings below to step through what it found and pin the one you want.

Chord voicings

Every chord in the stream, in order. Pick one, then step through its voicings — the list is ordered by how little your hand moves from the chord before it, so the first few are the smoothest. Auto hands the chord back to the search; a stepped choice is pinned, and the chords after it re-optimize around it.
