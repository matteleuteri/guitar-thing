Track

Drag a chord region to another bar (its old bar becomes a - rest), drag a note region to another string or column, or click any string lane to type a fret number at that beat — the notation rewrites itself.

Tools

Every chord in the stream, in order. When the same name lands on several bars, their tabs name the bar — and each occurrence can be pinned to a different voicing, so one C can sit as x32010 in one bar and xx0212 in another. Pick one, then step through its voicings — the list is ordered by how little your hand moves from the chord before it, so the first few are the smoothest. Auto hands the chord back to the search; a stepped choice is pinned, and the chords after it re-optimize around it.

Chords

Directives and tab lanes only — the chord stream is laid out on the Track (each event is its bar). Every character in a lane is one column: -/space rest, x mute, a number is a fret, and a two-digit fret occupies two columns, so it needs a matching dash in the other lanes (the app pads and warns when one lane runs long). Clicking a string lane on the track also writes into these lanes.

How to write this — syntax, the bar ruler, and what plays
  • A chord line is one chord per bar, read left to right. Spaces or | both work, and the spacing is yours to choose — see the bar ruler below. A - as a chord is a rest bar: no chord that bar, but the bar is kept (that's what a chord dragged off its bar leaves behind).
  • A tab lane is LANE| plus the notes, one per character column: e|5---7---|. Lanes are e B G D A E.
  • They play together, not instead of each other. A chord and a tab note on the same beat sound as one moment: the chord with the note on top of it, exactly as you'd strum it. The "What you'll hear" list spells that out as C + 5 | xx201x | E3 G3 C4 with A4 on top.
  • The bar ruler above the box is the shared grid: each number sits at the character where that bar starts, two characters along (a tab column starts after the e|). Pad the chord line so its tokens land on those numbers and both layers line up — it changes nothing you hear, it just makes the alignment visible. C Am F G and C       Am are the same four chords.
  • Space, -, . and _ are rests. x is a muted string. A two-digit fret uses two columns, so 10 is wider than 9.
  • If the lanes are different lengths, the short ones are padded with rests and a warning appears in the readout — a bar can't shift sideways because every character is a column (watch a two-digit fret, it costs a column).
  • tempo 96 — bpm (bpm also works).
  • bar 4 — beats per bar. Only needed to change 4/4.
  • step eighths — how long one column lasts. Take it from your fastest note: quarter, eighths, sixteenths, eighth triplets, or a plain number of beats like 0.25. The reader names the grid back at you.
  • strum 55 — ms between strings. 0 blocks the chord.
  • release 400 — damp each note early. Omit it to let the ring, which is the default.
  • Everything is optional except the notes. Lines starting with # are comments.

A chord name is pitch classes only — C does not carry frets, so the app searches for a shape. Use the Chords tab to step through what it found and pin the one you want.
