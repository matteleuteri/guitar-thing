Note/Chord Finder

Give it a set of notes: it highlights every spot on the fretboard where those notes live, then finds chord fingerings (muted strings allowed) where every chord note is played and all fretted positions lie within a small fret span.

Give it a chord progression: it picks one voicing per chord and chains them so the whole song shifts as little as possible, highlighting the notes you can keep held as it changes.

Describe a riff and hear it back before you learn it. A chord stream plays one strummed chord per bar; a tab lane plays single notes on the beats you write. Every event is staggered by the strum width, so a block chord is a strum of 0 and an arpeggio is a wide one.

How to write this — syntax, the bar ruler, and what plays
  • A chord line is one chord per bar, read left to right. Spaces or | both work, and the spacing is yours to choose — see the bar ruler below.
  • A tab lane is LANE| plus the notes, one per character column: e|5---7---|. Lanes are e B G D A E.
  • They play together, not instead of each other. A chord and a tab note on the same beat sound as one moment: the chord with the note on top of it, exactly as you'd strum it. The "What you'll hear" list under the box spells that out as C + 5 → E3 G3 C4 with A4 on top.
  • The bar ruler above the box is the shared grid: each number sits at the character where that bar starts, two characters along (a tab column starts after the e|). Pad the chord line so its tokens land on those numbers and both layers line up — it changes nothing you hear, it just makes the alignment visible. C Am F G and C       Am are the same four chords.
  • Space, -, . and _ are rests. x is a muted string. A two-digit fret uses two columns, so 10 is wider than 9.
  • Every lane must be the same number of characters — pad the short ones with dashes, or a bar shifts sideways.
  • tempo 96 — bpm (bpm also works).
  • bar 4 — beats per bar. Only needed to change 4/4.
  • step eighths — how long one column lasts. Take it from your fastest note: quarter, eighths, sixteenths, eighth triplets, or a plain number of beats like 0.25. The reader below names the grid back at you.
  • strum 55 — ms between strings. 0 blocks the chord. Defaults to the Strum speed box above.
  • release 400 — damp each note early. Omit it to let the ring, which is the default.
  • Everything is optional except the notes. Lines starting with # are comments.
